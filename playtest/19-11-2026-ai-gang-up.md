# The AIs gang up on a runaway (19-11-2026)

Jacob, about a 7-player game at round 4 where one AI held 18 of the 30 territories: "it's very much unbalanced already, how would anyone recover from this (even the other AI players)?"

Mostly nobody did. A leader with 15 or more territories at round 5 went on to win 82–91% of games, and the other AIs barely went after it: cutting the leader's group was worth 0.06 per territory in the Normal AI's scoring. Jacob picked an AI fix over a rule change ("having the AI gang up a little instead of changing the rules").

**The change** (`src/ai/normal.ts`): the Normal AI treats an opponent holding at least 1.5× the average territories of the players still in, and at least 8, as a runaway. It adds +0.6 to the score of any attack on them, and each territory an attack cuts from the leader's largest group now counts 0.15 instead of 0.06. No rule changes, so there's nothing new in How to play and no save default: a save replays its action log without running the AI.

## Options measured first

Throwaway copies of the rules and the AI, 1,500 seven-player all-Normal games per option plus the gate's 1,000 duels and 1,000 eight-player games (18-11-2026, on the maps from before the dice-stacks merge):

| Option | Leader with 15+ at round 5: how often → wins | Eight-player p99 (limit 33) |
|---|---|---|
| Today | 16% → 86–91% | 27–28 |
| AIs gang up (this change) | 9–11% → 87–88% | 28–29 |
| Less income for big empires: full for the first 14 of the largest group, half beyond, off in the final | 16% → 78–84% | 31–32 |
| The same from 12 | 15% → 77–80% | 34–37, fails |
| Catch-up income: a player below the average gets +(average − own territories) dice a turn | 8% → 75% | 47, fails |
| Catch-up link bonus +4 instead of +2 | 24% → 79% | 28 |

The gang-up makes a runaway rarer, but one that has formed wins as often as before. The rule changes do the opposite, and every point they take off a formed runaway's win rate makes games longer. Turning the knockout bonus off changed nothing. A gentler gang-up (+0.4, with the leader weight left at 0.06) stopped fewer runaways on a second seed set (12% against 9%) for no length gain, so the +0.6 version shipped.

## What a game looks like

Seed 200027, seven Normal AIs, territories per seat after each round:
- **Before:** seat 0 goes 6 → 10 → 13 → 19 → 24 while the others spend 0–3 attacks a round on it and 7–22 elsewhere. It wins in round 8.
- **After:** the table hits whoever pulls ahead: 6 of 6 attacks in round 1, 10 of 14 in round 4. Nobody passes 14 territories by round 14, and the game runs to round 24.

Seed 200006 shows what the change doesn't fix. The leader still gets to 15 at round 4, partly because few of the others border it with good odds. It wins in round 9, one round later than before.
