# Shaken: a defender that holds (20-11-2026)

Jacob noticed that a stack that draws can attack again. Under `drawAttrition` a draw drops the attacker to the defender's dice, so an 8 that draws against a 7 keeps 7 and can try a neighbour, or the same defender again. He asked to freeze a stack that drew for the rest of the turn, and whether a defender that held its ground should be easier to beat.

**The rule** (`shaken`, a prototype, off by default; try it with `?shaken=on`): when the defender holds (a draw or a clear loss for the attacker), the attacking stack is spent and can't attack again that turn. The defender is shaken until the turn ends, so a tie against it goes to the attacker. Under the default loss rule only a draw leaves a stack with dice to attack again, so in practice each territory attacks at most once a turn.

## Measured first

Throwaway scripts in the scratchpad played all-AI games with a copy of the rules. The AIs knew the changed odds, so they went after a shaken defender on purpose. The copies reproduced `npm run sim -- --games 2000` exactly, and the copied AIs picked the same move as `chooseAction` every time. Every row ran on the CI seeds (1,000 Normal-vs-Easy duels, 1,000 eight-player games) plus a fresh set: 3,000 duels and 3,000 games each at 8 and 4 players, and 2,000 at 3.

**Freezing alone** changes nothing measurable. The AIs attack again from a stack that drew in 0.25–0.33% of all attacks, and attacking the same defender again is always worse than even odds, since a draw leaves you at most the defender's dice and ties go to the defender.

| Variant | Normal beats Easy (4,000 duels) | 8p p90 / p99, CI seeds (limit 28 / 36) | 8p avg rounds (fresh) | Smaller 4p finalist with 6+ wins | Attacks on a held defender |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 89.4% | 22 / 31 | 14.7 | 31.9% | – |
| Freeze only | 89.0% | 21 / 29 | 14.8 | 31.5% | – |
| Freeze + ties to the attacker after a draw | 89.0% | 22 / 29 | 14.6 | 34.6% | 0.9%, won 72% |
| **Freeze + ties to the attacker after any hold (built)** | **88.7%** | **22 / 33** | **14.5** | **35.1%** | **3.0%, won 68%** |
| Ties to the attacker after any hold, no freeze | 88.8% | 21 / 33, one game of 156 rounds | 14.4 | 34.4% | 4.4%, won 63% |
| Freeze + the held defender rolls one die fewer | 86.6% | 21 / 29; a fresh game hit the 1,000-round cap | 14.9 | 35.1% | 3.1%, won 83% |
| Freeze + the defender loses a die for good on a draw | 89.0% | 21 / 31 | 14.7 | 34.3% | 0.9%, won 87% |
| Freeze + the defender loses a die for good on any hold | 75.2% | 22 / 34; 16 games hit the cap | 17.5 | 31.7% | 3.1%, won 82% |

Noise is about ±0.5 points on the duels and ±1.8 on the finalist column. The built variant, in more detail (fresh seeds): 3-player games average 8.7 rounds instead of 9.0, and 4-player 10.4 instead of 10.6. The smaller finalist with 6+ territories wins 29.3% at 8 players (28.3% today) and 38.1% at 3 (36.9%). The round-5 leader wins 51.5% at 8 players (53.0%). A leader with 15+ territories at round 5 shows up in 496 of 3,000 games instead of 430 and wins 84% instead of 88%: the rule helps whoever attacks most, leaders too. Eight-player wins per seat run 11.1–14.0% (fair share 12.5%), as today.

What a tie is worth: equal stacks go from 44–47% to 53–56%, 5 against 4 from 72% to 78%, and 8 against 7 from 67% to 73%.

Jacob picked freeze plus shaken after any hold.

## Built

`npm run sim -- --games 2000 --shaken on` reproduces the prototype's CI numbers exactly: Normal beats Easy 886/1000 (88.6%); eight-player p50 14, p90 22, p99 33, max 47, none past 60; 333,242 attacks; 0 crashes, illegal moves or cap hits. 42,997 holds shook a defender; 10,266 attacks went at a shaken one and 6,497 of them won. Default rules are unchanged: 907/1000, p90 22, p99 31.

- Engine: `RulesConfig.shaken`; `GameState.spent` and `GameState.shaken`, cleared when a turn ends; a `shaken` event; `attackRolled` carries `shaken: true` when the defender was shaken, so a tie went to the attacker. `canAttackFrom` and `attackOdds` in `rules.ts`, and `winProbability(a, d, tiesToAttacker)`.
- The AIs read the odds through `attackOdds`, so Normal goes after a shaken defender at even dice.
- Board: a spent stack turns grey and can't be selected, and a shaken defender's dice count gets a broken amber outline. "Shaken!" floats over it in amber, and the odds badge counts the tie.
- Roll strip: "6 → 4 · spent" under the attacker and "shaken" under the defender, and "Tie · captured" when a tie goes to the attacker. On a phone, "kept 4 · shaken" was cut off, so the defender's line is just "shaken".
- Floating labels near the board's edge now stay on screen, and a label lifts above one already showing. "Shaken!" ran off the right edge of the rotated phone board and sat on top of the "−2" next to it.
- How to play explains it, with a tip, when the rule is on. The README mirrors the defaults, so it's unchanged.
- Saves from before replay without it. The end-of-game "biggest upset" uses the shaken odds for a tie win.
