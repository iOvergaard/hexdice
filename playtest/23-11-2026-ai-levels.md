# Three AI levels: the old Normal is Hard, and a new Normal slips up (23-11-2026)

Jacob found that Normal plays with no randomness: it never takes a bad attack and never stops while a good one is left, so only the dice beat it. He asked whether a Hard AI (issue #13) would bring anything, or whether Normal should become Hard with a new level in between. He picked "rename plus Mix25" from the options below.

## Change

- **Hard** (`src/ai/hard.ts`) is the old Normal, move for move: odds of at least 50% (30% from a full 8), captures that grow its group, break the leader's or knock a player out, and ganging up on runaways.
- **Normal** (`src/ai/normal.ts`) plays Hard's move, except that a quarter of its moves (`NORMAL_SLIP = 0.25`) are Easy's: a random attack with any chance of winning, or (1 time in 10) an early end of turn. Its other moves still gang up on runaways, so the runaway crosshair shows whenever a Normal or Hard computer player is left to go after them.
- Title: AI level chips **Easy · Normal · Hard**, Normal by default. A remembered choice of `normal` and `?ai=normal` links now mean the new Normal; `?ai=hard` gives the old one.
- Saves go to version 2. A version 1 save (from before) loads with its Normal players as Hard, so a game in progress keeps its opponents. Copy game records say `v: 2`.
- `npm run sim`: the duels are split, half Hard against Easy (the first half of the old seeds) and half Normal against Easy. Both must win at least 70%. The 8-player length gate plays all-Hard tables, the longest, so its numbers don't change.
- E2E tests that rely on a seeded game's ending (`copy-game`, `game-over`) now ask for `ai=hard`, so they keep the games they were written for. New: `tests/e2e/ai-levels.spec.ts`.

## The numbers

A throwaway script (scratchpad, not committed) played the candidates. Its copy of Normal matched `chooseNormal` at every move in 200 games. "Mix25" is exactly the new Normal. Today's rules, 1,000 duels and 1,200 four-player games per row:

| Level | 1v1 against Easy | One seat against 3 Easy | One seat against 3 Hard (fair 25%) | 8 of them: p90 / p99 rounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| focus-to4 (issue #13) | 89.0% | 71.3% | 41.4% | 32 / 41 |
| **Hard** (old Normal) | 87.7% | 61.0% | 25% | 21 / 32 |
| **Normal** (Mix25) | 81.6% | 51.4% | 15.8% | 19 / 27 |
| Greedy (best odds only) | 82.0% | 48.7% | 14.8% | 18 / 25 |
| Mix40 | 78.4% | 43.3% | 13.8% | 18 / 25 |
| Timid (stops 15% early) | 79.1% | 52.8% | 16.9% | (turns drag: 7.8 rounds a duel) |
| Noisy (score ±0.25, attacks from 40%) | 88.9% | 57.3% | 21.0% | 16 / 22 |

- Hard is not optimal: focus-to4 beats a table of Hard players 41% of the time. Eight of them run past the length limits (p90 32 > 28, p99 41 > 36), so it stays parked.
- Duels separate the levels less than tables: the dice decide a lot one against one. Against Easy tables the ladder is about 51% → 61% → 71%.
- Greedy is about as strong as Mix25, but it makes the same blind-spot mistake every time. Jacob picked Mix25.
- Side finding, not acted on: Noisy beat Hard 54.0% to 46.0% in 1,000 duels. Attacking from 40% may pay when only two are left.
